# How to group ads into angles

## What counts as an angle

The angle is the main reason to buy that the ad leads with. One product can be sold on many angles. Ask of each ad: **"Who is this for, and what is it promising them?"**

Angles usually fall into one of these families. Use them to think, and name the angle in the brand's own terms.

| Family | The ad leads with | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | A pain the viewer has now | "Bloated by 3pm every day" |
| Side effect or trigger | A new situation that creates a need | "Constipated since starting a GLP-1" |
| Outcome | The result they want | "Hair growing back at the temples" |
| Identity | Who they are | "For dads who lift" |
| Comparison | Why this beats the usual choice | "Stop buying it on Amazon" |
| Mechanism | How it works, the new thing | "The only one with 10 billion CFU that survive stomach acid" |
| Proof | Evidence first | "47,000 five-star reviews" |
| Offer | The deal | "Free starter kit today" |
| Occasion | A moment or season | "The gift she'll actually use" |
| Objection | A doubt answered | "I thought greens powders tasted like grass" |
| Story | A person's before and after | "I was the tired mum" |
| Authority | An expert's word | "What a dermatologist uses" |

## Steps

1. **Read the whole ad.** The hook can mislead. A video may open with a joke and sell on a medical problem. Use the transcript first, then the headline and primary text.
2. **One main angle per creative.** If an ad argues two, pick the one it spends the most words on, and note the second only if it recurs.
3. **Name it the way a buyer would say it.** "GLP-1 constipation", never "Side effect or trigger". Family names are for you.
4. **Merge.** Start specific, then merge angles that make the same promise to the same person. Aim for 4 to 10 per brand. More than 10 means you are splitting hairs; fewer than 4 on a large account means you merged too far.
5. **Check with numbers.** For each angle total the creatives, the estimated share, the longest run and the count launched in the last 14 days.
6. **Sanity check.** Read three ads from each angle again. If one does not belong, move it.

## Reading the totals

| Pattern | Status |
|---|---|
| Few creatives, high share, long run | Proven, few new creatives |
| Many creatives, high share, long run | Proven, with new creatives still launching |
| Many new creatives, low share | Being tested |
| High share, all new | Give the share, rank and creative age; no label |
| Old creatives, low share, recent kills | Fading |
| Present only in killed ads | Tried and dropped |

## Ads with no transcript

Judge them on headline, primary text and page type, and count them with the rest. Say nothing about which ads had a transcript.

## Things to avoid

- Calling a format an angle. "UGC" and "founder video" are formats.
- Calling an offer an angle unless the ad leads with the deal.
- Counting copies as separate creatives.
- Calling an angle proven for its number of creatives. Status comes from share and days live.
